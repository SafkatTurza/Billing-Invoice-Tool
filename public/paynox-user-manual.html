01

About Paynox

Paynox is a complete billing and company-finance system for DreamCore Studio. It creates and manages professional documents — invoices, estimates, purchase orders, work orders and money receipts — and runs a full finance back office alongside them: requisitions, vouchers, expenses, bills, payroll, ledgers and reports.

Everything runs inside your web browser. There is no server to log into and no data sent to the cloud — your records live on the computer you use Paynox on, and you move them between machines with a backup file (see §13). That makes Paynox fast and private, and it makes one habit important: keep backups.

What Paynox gives you

  • Billing documents with line items, tax and discount rules, amount-in-words, brand styling, and export to PDF, Excel and Word.
  • A finance module that turns approved paperwork into a real ledger — so dashboards, reports and statements reflect money that has actually been approved and moved.
  • Role-based access so each person sees and does exactly what their job allows.
  • An approval chain with real signatures, segregation of duties, and a full audit trail on every document.
  • Payroll — employees, salary sheets, payslips, disbursement and certificates — and a loans & advances register with a permanent audit history.
Good to know

Paynox is fully responsive. On a phone or tablet the sidebar becomes a slide-in drawer you open with the menu button (☰) in the top bar. Everything in this manual works the same on every screen size.

Roles & permissions

Every user has one of four roles. Your role decides which menu items you see, which documents you can open, and whether you can create, approve, or only view. Menu items you have no access to are simply hidden.

Super Admin SA

The system owner — created once, at setup.

  • Everything, everywhere
  • Manage all users & security
  • Finance data entry and final sign-off
  • Finance masters, backups, audit log

Admin Management

Management — reviews and authorises.

  • All billing documents
  • Views finance & gives final sign-off
  • Cannot create or edit finance entries
  • Manages Accounts & Business users

Accounts Finance

The finance department — prepares & records.

  • Creates all finance documents
  • Signs preparer / checker slots
  • Cannot give management final sign-off
  • Sees its own estimates only

Business Team Sales

Client-facing sales staff.

  • Invoices & estimates only
  • No purchase/work orders or receipts
  • No finance access at all
  • No revenue figures; own estimates only

Why finance is locked down

All finance data entry is reserved for Accounts and Super Admin. Admin is management: it can view and give the final approval, but never create or edit finance records. This separation is deliberate — it keeps preparation and authorisation in different hands.

03

First-time setup

The very first time Paynox opens on a computer, it runs a one-time Setup Wizard to create the Super Admin account. You only do this once per installation.

  1. Enter the Super Admin's full name.
  2. Set a security answer (used later for password recovery — remember it exactly, including spelling).
  3. Select Create Super Admin Account. Paynox generates your User ID and a temporary password and shows them once.
  4. Copy both down, then choose Continue to Login.
  5. Sign in with the generated credentials. Paynox immediately asks you to set your own password before you can go further.
Write it down

The generated password is shown only once. If you lose it before setting your own, you'll need the security answer to recover the account. Your User ID looks like DCS-A1B2C3.

After setup you land on the dashboard as Super Admin. From there you create the rest of your team under Settings → Users (see §12).

04

Signing in & account recovery

Logging in

Sign in with your User ID (for example DCS-A1B2C3) and your password. If an administrator has just created or reset your account, Paynox asks you to set a new password on first login.

Requesting an account

New staff can request access from the login screen via Sign up. This creates a pending account — it doesn't work until an administrator approves it and assigns a role under Settings → Users. The person also sets a security question during signup, for recovery.

Forgot your password

  1. Choose Forgot password on the login screen.
  2. Enter your User ID and answer your security question.
  3. Set a new password and sign in.

Changing your password anytime

Once signed in, update your password under Settings → My Account.

While you work

Session warning. Sessions last 30 days; Paynox shows a banner about five minutes before one expires so you can extend it without losing work.

Document locking. When you open a document to edit, it locks for everyone else — they see it read-only and can send a request to edit. Locks auto-release after 30 minutes of inactivity, and an administrator can force-unlock. Your in-progress edits are also kept as a recoverable draft.

05

The dashboard

The dashboard is your landing page and it adapts to your role. You'll typically see:

  • Document count cards — how many invoices, estimates and so on you have.
  • Financial summary — grouped by currency and never mixed or auto-converted, so figures stay honest. Hidden for Business Team.
  • Quick-create buttons for the documents you're allowed to make.
  • Recent documents for fast re-entry.
  • Outstanding payables with an overdue count, once you use Bills (see §7).

The top bar carries global search (it spans billing and finance documents and jumps you straight to the right page), a notifications bell, and log out.

06

Billing documents

Invoices, Estimates, Purchase Orders, Work Orders and Money Receipts all share the same rhythm: pick a type, fill the form, preview, then export or print.

Concepts that apply to every document

  • Numbering. Each type is numbered PREFIX-YYYYMMDD-SERIAL. The serial is reserved the first time you save, so abandoned drafts never leave gaps, and counters never reset.
  • Auto-save & drafts. Work is saved as you go, with a save-status indicator; an interrupted edit can be recovered.
  • Export. Every document exports to Excel (Summary / Line Items / Signatures sheets) and Word, and prints to PDF in A4 portrait — all generated in your browser.
  • PDF templates. Choose Modern, Simple or Flexible under Settings → Document Style; the choice, along with your brand colour and font, applies to every document.
  • Attachments. Supporting files attach to a document (see limits in §13).

Invoices & Estimates

Add line items with quantity and rate. The amount lock/unlock rule lets you type a line total directly or let it calculate — whichever you lock, the other follows. Pricing supports Discount, AIT and VAT (VAT only appears once AIT is in play), and the total is written out as amount-in-words in both international and Bangladesh (Lakh/Crore) styles. Add bank details and dynamic signature labels as needed.

Visibility

Estimates are private to their creator for Accounts and Business Team — one can't see the other's estimates. Administrators see all of them.

Mark as Paid

On an invoice, Mark as Paid records the payment and auto-generates a short receipt (RCPT-…), notifying the creator and administrators.

Purchase Orders & Work Orders

These name a vendor and add a specification column. Payment is broken into milestones with a running total indicator that confirms your milestones add up to 100%.

Money Receipts

A client-facing acknowledgement capturing payment and transaction details. (Keep this distinct from the internal Cash Receipt Voucher in finance — see §7.)

07

The finance module

The finance module is the company's back office. Its guiding rule is simple: only approved paperwork that actually moves money posts to the ledger — so every dashboard, report and statement reflects reality, not intentions.

Who can do what

Creating and editing finance records is limited to Accounts and Super Admin. Admin can view and give final sign-off. Business Team has no finance access.

Accounts & heads (the foundation)

Two masters, set up by Super Admin under Settings → Finance Accounts, feed every finance form:

  • Accounts — your cash, bank and mobile-money (MFS) accounts. This is the Deposited To / paid-from list. Paynox ships with Cash in Hand, Bank Account — Current, bKash and Nagad; add your own real accounts and branches.
  • Heads — your chart of income and expense categories. Income heads (the Income Head list) include Sales, Service, Consulting, Rental, Interest, Commission and Other Income; expense heads cover operations, admin, utilities, transport, salary, marketing and more.
Set opening balances

Each account starts at an opening balance of zero. Enter each account's real opening figure in Settings → Finance Accounts so the ledger, statements and trial balance reflect your true cash position from day one.

Requisitions

A request to spend, approved in order: Proposed → Checked → Authorised. A voucher can later link to the requisition it settles.

Vouchers

The voucher is the workhorse. One document, three types chosen with a toggle:

  • Payment — money out (navy template).
  • Cash — physical cash, in either direction: Cash Payment (out) or Cash Receipt (in) (green template).
  • Debit — a debit voucher (teal template).

Key behaviours:

  • Booked once. A voucher is either the Primary transaction (it posts to the ledger) or a Linked / Internal Record (documentation only, no extra financial impact). Linked records share a Transaction ID like TXN-20260722-001 and can never double-post.
  • Payment modes — Cash, Bank Transfer, BEFTN, Cheque, Card, Online/MFS, Others, each with its own reference field. Account and card numbers are masked in print (e.g. •••• 1234).
  • Split by head — a voucher can split across expense heads, each line posting to its own head, and can record a cheque number.
  • Signatures — the standard four-slot chain, expandable to an optional fifth signatory.
  • Numbering — PV-…, CV-…, DV-… per type, resetting yearly.

A Transaction Detail view groups the primary voucher, every linked record and the posted ledger entries, so the one real financial impact is auditable at a glance.

Daily Expenses

Record day-to-day spend straight to the ledger, attach the receipt, and pick the expense head and account it came from.

Bills & Payables

Record vendor bills you owe, with a due date and an optional attached invoice. No cash moves on save — you record payments against the bill (full or partial), and only those payments post to the ledger. Bills show Open / Partial / Paid, an outstanding balance and an overdue aging bucket (0–30 / 31–60 / 61–90 / 90+ days).

Income & Investment

Record money in — service income or capital/investment injections — straight to the ledger, so reports see the full picture rather than expenses alone. Each entry names an Income Head and the Deposited To account, and can carry a printable receipt. Investment is tracked separately from revenue.

Recurring entries

Templates for monthly costs (rent, internet, subscriptions) that spawn a Daily Expense or a Bill on a chosen day. Nothing posts automatically — a due entry shows a Post now button so a person stays in control, and each template remembers the last month it ran so it can't double-post.

Budgets

Set a monthly cap per expense head. The Budgets page compares the cap to this month's actual posted spend (vouchers, daily expenses and bill payments alike), with a bar that turns amber at 80% and red when a head goes over.

Corrections & housekeeping

  • Account transfers (contra) — move money between your own accounts (Cash → Bank) from the Ledger. Posts a matched out+in pair and is excluded from income/expense figures.
  • Void / reverse — reverse an approved voucher, requisition, expense or income (reason required). The ledger entries void and the item leaves all figures, but the record stays, marked Reversed, for audit.
  • Duplicate-as-new — clone any finance document into a fresh draft with number, signatures and status cleared.
  • Finance recycle bin — deleted finance documents keep for 30 days with Restore and permanent delete.

Multiple currencies

Vouchers, income, bills and requisitions can be recorded in a foreign currency. Because rates change daily, you capture a per-document BDT exchange rate; the printed document shows the original amount and its BDT equivalent. Consolidated figures combine every currency into one BDT total, while per-currency ledgers and statements keep their original currency.

08

Approvals & signatures

Finance documents move through a signature chain. A document is only Approved once the management signature is applied.

Your signature

Store your own signature — draw it or upload it — under Settings → My Account. When it's your turn, you apply that signature to your slot with a date you set.

The rules that keep it honest

  • In order. Slots are signed sequentially; management (CEO/MD) signs last.
  • Segregation of duties. The person who prepares a document can't check or approve it, and no one signs two slots. The maker may only sign the first (preparer) slot.
  • Reject — an approver can reject (terminal, reason required); the preparer is notified and can duplicate it as a fresh draft.
  • Send back for correction — clears all signatures and returns the document to the preparer as an editable draft.
  • Timeline. Every document carries a visible history — submitted, each signature, sent-back/rejected with reason, approved, reversed — with who and when.
  • Notifications. Submitting or signing pings whoever can act next.
Threshold routing (optional)

Under Settings → Approval Rules you can set an amount gate. Below the threshold, the checker can finalise on their own and the management slot is optional; at or above it — or with routing off — the management final signature is required. Segregation of duties always applies on top.

External receivers

A voucher can be flagged as paid to an outside party against a money receipt. The chain reorders so the receiver is confirmed before final sign-off: Accountant → Checked By → Received Payment → Approved By. The money receipt is attached and reviewed by the approver before approval.

09

Payroll & salary

Employees

An employee master with IDs like EMP-001 — name, department, designation, base salary, employment type and bank details (kept on the profile, never printed on a payslip).

Salary sheets

A salary sheet ("Employee Remuneration Requisition") is pre-filled from your active employees. Add configurable earning and deduction components, and it follows the same signature chain (Prepared → Checked → Authorised). On approval, the salary posts to the ledger and monthly expenditure.

Payroll tracking (optional per sheet)

Turn on tracking to capture, per employee, present days, unpaid-leave days and overtime (hours × rate). Unpaid leave is pro-rated on base salary using the sheet's working days; overtime adds to pay. Extra columns appear only when the sheet actually uses them, so older sheets stay clean.

Loan installments on the sheet

One click — Auto-fill loan installments — pulls each employee's due installment (capped at their outstanding balance) onto the sheet. On approval, those installments are recorded against the loans and balances drop. Repayments are stamped with the sheet they came from, so re-approving a sheet never double-counts.

Payslips & more

  • Payslips ("Payroll Receipt Copy") — one per employee from an approved sheet, itemising overtime, unpaid leave and loan repayment, with a Year-to-Date block. Printable to PDF, one A4 page each.
  • Bulk payslips — every payslip for a sheet, one per page, in a single print run.
  • Salary disbursement voucher — auto-generated per approved sheet (one payee line per employee); derived from the sheet, so it never re-posts.
  • Salary certificate — a per-employee employment/salary certificate with designation, tenure, current breakdown and YTD paid.
Order of operations

Payslips are generated only after a salary sheet is approved by higher management. Prepare and get the sheet signed first; the payslips and disbursement voucher follow from it.

10

Loans & advances

A register of money advanced to staff, each with a principal and a monthly installment. Installments are usually recovered automatically through salary sheets (§9), but you can also record a manual settlement.

Recording a settlement / recovery

Besides salary deductions, record a repayment by cash, bank transfer, cheque, adjustment, write-off and more — with a reference, remarks and supporting attachments (JPG, PNG or PDF). New settlements are capped at the outstanding balance.

Every money change is traceable

Any action that moves money keeps a permanent record. A reason is required when you change the original principal, change an existing settlement amount, or reverse a settlement; adjustments and write-offs always require a note.

Each loan carries an Activity & audit history that stores the previous and new amounts, who made the change, and when. The original values are never silently overwritten.

11

Reports & insight

Because approved paperwork posts to a real ledger, the reports write themselves. All are grouped by currency and export to Excel and PDF.

Monthly Expenditure report

Approved expenses for the month, with billing income shown alongside. Pending items are listed separately and never inflate the figures.

Financial Reports pack

A print-ready pack for This Month / This Year / All Time:

  • Cash Flow Statement — inflows (billing income, recorded income, investment) vs outflows (approved expenses), with net cash flow.
  • Profit & Loss — revenue − expenses = net profit/loss (investment is treated as financing and excluded).
  • Actual Expense Summary — expense-by-head, straight from the ledger.
  • Receivables Aging — unpaid invoices bucketed 0–30 / 31–60 / 61–90 / 90+ days.

General Ledger

  • Trial Balance — a balancing debit/credit sheet as of any date, with a Balanced / Out-of-balance badge.
  • Ledger by Head — every posting against a chosen head over a date range, with a running net.
  • Account Statement — a bank-statement-style run for any account: opening balance, each movement with a running balance, and a closing balance.

Insights & Forecast

  • Income vs Expense trend — a 6- or 12-month chart with a net line.
  • Where the money goes — expense-by-head as proportional bars.
  • Cash-flow forecast — projects your running cash balance forward (recurring templates repeat, open bills land in their due month, unpaid invoices are expected in theirs). A planning view only — nothing here posts to the ledger.

13

Data, backup & safety

Paynox keeps your data in the browser on the machine you use it on. There's no cloud copy, so backups are how you stay safe and how you move between computers.

Backups

  1. Go to Settings → Data Backup.
  2. Choose Export to download a file named DCS_Billing_Backup_YYYY-MM-DD.json.
  3. To restore or move machines, choose Import and select a backup file.
Import replaces everything

Importing a backup overwrites all current data in that browser. Only import into an empty or intentionally-replaced installation, and export the current state first if you're unsure.

Make it a habit

Export a backup on a regular schedule — end of week is a good rhythm — and keep the file somewhere safe. Clearing browser data, switching browsers, or a new computer all start Paynox fresh.

Attachments

Bills, receipts and scanned copies are stored in the browser's IndexedDB (separate from the main data), capped at 2 MB per file with automatic image compression, multiple files per document, and a storage-usage meter — sized for roughly 50–60 scans a month. Attachments are included conceptually with the document but are held in that separate store.

Audit Log & Recycle Bin

  • Audit Log SA Admin — a running record of significant actions.
  • Recycle Bin SA Admin — soft-deleted documents (billing and finance) kept 30 days, with Restore and permanent delete.

Glossary

  • Posts to the ledger — the entry now counts in dashboards, reports and statements. Only approved, money-moving items do this.
  • Primary vs Linked voucher — the primary carries the real financial impact; a linked record is documentation sharing the same Transaction ID, with no extra impact.
  • Segregation of duties — the maker can't also approve; deciding signatures come from someone else.
  • Contra / account transfer — money moved between your own accounts; excluded from income and expense.
  • Reversed — an approved item undone; its ledger effect is voided but the record is kept for audit.
  • Head — an income or expense category in your chart of accounts.
  • MFS — mobile financial services (bKash, Nagad).

Getting the most from Paynox

  • Set real opening balances on your accounts before you trust the statements.
  • Enter the BDT rate on every foreign-currency document so consolidated totals stay accurate.
  • Prefer Send back for correction over deleting — it preserves the audit trail.
  • Export a backup regularly, and always before importing one.
15

Calculations & formulas

This appendix documents every calculation Paynox performs — from the tax and VAT on an invoice to the numbers behind each report. It's written for two readers at once: a plain-language explanation for everyday users, and the exact formula and the code function that produces it for technical staff and auditors.

How to read this section

Every calculation shows a function() · file tag naming the exact function in the source and the file it lives in. All calculations run in your browser; nothing is sent anywhere. Every currency is kept separate — figures are never auto-converted or mixed across currencies.

Two rules used everywhere

  • Safe numbers. Any blank or non-numeric field is read as 0 before it enters a formula (Number(value) || 0), so an empty box never breaks a total.
  • Two-decimal rounding. Money results are rounded to two decimals with Math.round(n × 100) / 100 (written as round2), so totals don't drift by fractions of a paisa.

15.1 · Invoice & estimate totals — Discount, Tax (AIT) and VAT

calcTotals(doc) · lineAmount(item) · isAmountLocked(item) — src/lib/pricing.js

In plain words — Paynox adds up the line items to a subtotal, takes off any discount, then applies Tax (AIT) and VAT on top. VAT is charged on the amount after AIT has been added, which is why the two always travel together — VAT only appears once AIT is switched on.

The exact math

# Each line's amount lineAmount = (rate > 0) ? (qty > 0 ? qty × rate : rate) # rate drives & locks the amount : manualAmount # no rate → you type the amount subtotal = Σ lineAmount # sum of every line discountAmount = subtotal × (discountRate / 100) afterDiscount = subtotal − discountAmount aitAmount = afterDiscount × (aitRate / 100) # Tax / AIT vatAmount = (afterDiscount + aitAmount) × (vatRate / 100) # VAT is on (base + AIT) # vatAmount = 0 whenever AIT is off grandTotal = afterDiscount + aitAmount + vatAmount
Worked example

Subtotal 1,000, Discount 10%, AIT 5%, VAT 15%:

Discount = 1,000 × 10% = 100 → After discount = 900
AIT = 900 × 5% = 45
VAT = (900 + 45) × 15% = 945 × 15% = 141.75
Grand total = 900 + 45 + 141.75 = 1,086.75

The amount lock rule

On each line, if you enter a rate the Amount is calculated and locked (isAmountLocked is true); with no rate you type the Amount directly. A rate with a blank/zero quantity is treated as a single unit, so Amount = rate.

The grand total is also spelled out as amount-in-words in both international and Bangladeshi (Lakh/Crore) styles. amountInWords.js

15.2 · Milestone check (Purchase & Work Orders)

milestoneStatus(milestones) — src/lib/pricing.js

In plain words — payment milestones on an order must add up to exactly 100%. Paynox totals the percentages and shows a colour: under 100% (amber), over 100% (red), exactly 100% (green).

total = Σ milestone.percentage state = total < 100 ? "under" # amber : total > 100 ? "over" # red : "exact" # green — good to go

15.3 · Voucher amount

voucherTotal(doc) — src/lib/finance.js

In plain words — if a voucher is split across several expense heads, its amount is the sum of those lines; otherwise it's the single amount you entered.

voucherTotal = lines.length ? Σ line.amount : amount

15.4 · Bills & payables — paid, due, status & aging

billPaid · billDue · billPayStatus · billOverdue · billAgeBucket — src/lib/finance.js

In plain words — a bill's outstanding balance is what you owe minus what you've already paid against it. From that Paynox derives whether the bill is Open, Partially paid or Paid, whether it's overdue, and how old the debt is.

billPaid = Σ payment.amount billDue = max(0, amount − billPaid) status = (paid ≥ amount) ? "Paid" : (paid > 0) ? "Partial" : "Open" overdue = billDue > 0 AND dueDate < today # Aging buckets, by days since the due date: # Not due · 0–30 · 31–60 · 61–90 · 90+

15.5 · Payroll — gross & net pay

lineComputed(line, sheet) · sheetTotals(doc) · ytdForEmployee(...) — src/lib/salary.js

In plain words — each employee's pay starts from their base salary. Overtime and any earning components are added to make gross; unpaid-leave days, deduction components and any loan installment are taken off to make net (take-home). Unpaid leave is charged at a per-day rate based on the sheet's working days (26 by default).

perDay = base / workingDays # workingDays defaults to 26 unpaidDeduction = perDay × unpaidLeaveDays overtimeAmount = overtimeHours × overtimeRate gross = base + overtimeAmount + earningComponents net = gross − unpaidDeduction − deductionComponents − loanDeduction

A salary sheet's totals (sheetTotals) simply add every employee's line together; year-to-date figures (ytdForEmployee) sum the approved sheets across the year.

15.6 · Loans & advances — outstanding & installment

loanRepaid · loanOutstanding · suggestedInstallment — src/lib/loans.js

In plain words — the balance still owed is the principal minus everything repaid. The installment auto-filled onto a salary sheet is the loan's monthly figure, but never more than the balance that's left.

loanRepaid = Σ repayment.amount loanOutstanding = round2( max(0, principal − loanRepaid) ) settled = status == "closed" OR loanOutstanding ≤ 0 suggestedInstallment = min(loan.installment, loanOutstanding)

15.7 · Reports — the ledger math

Every report is built from the posted ledger — only approved, money-moving entries. Paynox uses simple single-entry cash accounting: money-in adds to an account and credits an income head; money-out subtracts from an account and debits an expense head; internal transfers move cash between your own accounts and touch no head.

Account statement (bank-statement style)

accountStatement(ledger, account, {from, to}) — src/lib/reports.js

Starts from the account's opening balance plus everything posted before the date range, then adds each movement to give a running balance and a closing figure.

opening = account.openingBalance + Σ (movements before "from") running balance += (direction == "in" ? +amount : −amount) # row by row closing = opening + Σ (in-range movements)

Ledger by head

ledgerByHead(ledger, headId, {from, to}) — src/lib/reports.js

Lists every posting against one category (head) in a date range with a running net.

runningNet = Σ in − Σ out # per currency

Trial balance

trialBalance(ledger, accounts, heads, asOf) — src/lib/reports.js

A debit/credit sheet as of any date. Cash/bank balances and expenses sit on the debit side; income, investment and opening-balance equity on the credit side. The sheet is balanced when the two sides match (to within half a paisa).

accounts & expenses → debit income, investment & opening equity → credit balanced = |totalDebit − totalCredit| < 0.05

Monthly trends

monthlyTrends(ledger, invoices, currency, months) — src/lib/reports.js

Income, investment and expense per month over the last 6 or 12 months, with a net line. Income counts real money-in plus paid billing invoices; investment is tracked separately from revenue.

per month: net = income + investment − expense income = (money-in that isn't investment) + paid invoices' grandTotal expense = Σ money-out

Cash-flow forecast

cashFlowForecast({ledger, accounts, recurring, bills, invoices}, currency, months) — src/lib/reports.js

A planning view only — it projects your cash balance forward and posts nothing. It starts from today's real cash, then each future month adds expected inflows (unpaid invoices in their due month) and subtracts expected outflows (recurring templates every month + open bills in their due month).

startBalance = Σ account openings + Σ posted (in − out) per month: net = inflow − outflow runningBalance += net outflow = recurring templates + open bills due that month inflow = unpaid invoices due that month
Where the report figures come from

The Profit & Loss, Cash Flow Statement and Expense Summary in the Financial Reports pack (§11) are all built on these same posted-ledger sums — revenue minus expenses for P&L, inflows minus outflows for cash flow, and expense-by-head for the summary. Investment is treated as financing and kept out of the profit figure.

For auditors & developers

Every function named here is a pure calculation — same inputs always give the same result, with no hidden state — which makes each one straightforward to verify or unit-test in isolation. The pricing math lives in src/lib/pricing.js, finance document math in src/lib/finance.js, payroll in src/lib/salary.js, loans in src/lib/loans.js, and all report math in src/lib/reports.js.
